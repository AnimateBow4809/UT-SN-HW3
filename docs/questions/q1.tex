	\questiontext{Optimizing Structural Balance in a Competitive Network}
	
	\begin{subquestion}{Using a triadic (triangle-based) analysis, identify the structural patterns that are inconsistent with the definition of strong structural balance. In your answer, specify examples of problematic triangles and describe the sign configuration of their edges.}
		\answer{
			If a complete graph satisfies strong structural balance, it must be possible to divide the nodes into two clusters such that all relationships within each cluster are positive, while all relationships between clusters are negative. Structural patterns that are inconsistent with strong structural balance arise when the product of the signs of the edges in a triangle is negative. 
			
			This occurs, for example, when a triangle contains two positive edges and one negative edge, or when all three edges are negative. Such triangles are considered unbalanced. If any unbalanced triangles exist in a graph, the graph cannot be strongly balanced.
			
			For instance, in the given graph, some examples of unbalanced triangles are:
			\begin{enumerate}
				\item The triangle $BCD$ is unbalanced since $CD$ and $CB$ are positive, while $DB$ is negative, resulting in a negative product of signs.
				\item The triangle $ABD$ is weakly balanced, since all of its edges are negative, again producing a negative product of signs.
				\item The triangle $ACD$ is unbalanced because $AC$ and $CD$ are positive, while $AD$ is negative, yielding a negative product.
			\end{enumerate}
			
			There are many additional examples of unbalanced triangles in this graph. In the next subquestion, we discuss why these triangles arise and how they can be eliminated.
		}
	\end{subquestion}
	
	\begin{subquestion}{The objective is to transform the network into a fully balanced two-faction structure, while minimizing the number of sign changes on edges.}
		\begin{enumerate}
			\item Determine which edge or edges must change their sign in order for the network to achieve strong structural balance.
			\answer{
				To address this question, we attempted to partition the graph into two clusters. As established by structural balance theory, a strongly balanced graph can be divided into two groups such that relationships within each group are positive and relationships between the groups are negative.
				
				Following this approach, we partitioned the graph into the clusters $\{A, B, C\}$ and $\{D, E, F\}$. We found that the minimum number of edge sign changes required to achieve strong structural balance is two. The required changes are:

				\end{enumerate}
			}
